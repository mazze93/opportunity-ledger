# Scholarly pipeline

The site is the source of truth. Everything else points back to it.

```
mazzeleczzare.com page ──► DOI (Rogue Scholar or Zenodo) ──► ORCID (automatic) ──► proof bank entry
         ▲                                                                                 │
         └──────────────── fix the site first when the outside record disagrees ◄──────────┘
```

Each kind of work has one route. Taking two routes for the same work makes duplicates on ORCID, which groups works only by DOI, ISBN or ISSN.

| Kind of work | Site page | DOI from | Reaches ORCID by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Essay or field note | `/blog/<slug>/` | Rogue Scholar (Crossref), from `/feed.json` | Crossref auto-update |
| Software release | the project's page or subdomain | Zenodo (DataCite), from a GitHub release | DataCite auto-update |
| Paper (PDF) outside the blog | `/papers/<name>.pdf` with a landing page | Zenodo upload, or none | DataCite auto-update, or `orcid/works.bib` |
| Credit on someone else's record (CVE, talk, byline) | a line on `/work/` | none; the outside record is the identifier | `orcid/works.bib` |

## One-time setup

Done once. Each step is his to take; none is automatic.

1. **Blog:** merge the `scholarly/rogue-scholar-feed` pull request in mazze-leczzare-blog. It adds the CC BY 4.0 notice, the full-text `/feed.json` with his ORCID iD, and citation metadata on every post. Done: merged 2026-11-14 (#223).
2. **Rogue Scholar:** submit the join form ([rogue-scholar.org](https://rogue-scholar.org), "Register your blog"). The form asks for name, email, ORCID iD, a description, the homepage URL, seven yes/no eligibility statements, and an OpenAlex field and subfield; Rogue Scholar finds the feed from the homepage. Submitted 2026-11-18 with his approval (field Computer Science, subfield Artificial Intelligence). Submissions are reviewed once a month, and the name and homepage are posted in the Rogue Scholar Slack for two weeks of comments. No DOIs until it is accepted. The form does not ask for a feed URL, and the homepage advertises two feeds: `/feed.json` (full text, held posts excluded) and `/rss.xml` (summaries only, every post including held ones). Rogue Scholar's FAQ does not say which it picks, so tell them to use `/feed.json` when they reply, and check the feed URL on the blog's Rogue Scholar page once it is listed.
3. **Crossref permission:** after the first DOI is registered, ORCID sends an inbox notice asking to let Crossref update the record. Grant it once.
4. **DataCite permission:** at [commons.datacite.org](https://commons.datacite.org), sign in with ORCID, authorise DataCite, and enable ORCID Auto-Update.
5. **Zenodo:** connect GitHub and switch on each repository listed in `orcid/CHECKLIST.md` section 8.
6. **ORCID by hand:** import `orcid/works.bib` and work through `orcid/CHECKLIST.md`.

## Each new essay

1. Write and publish the post as usual. It is CC BY 4.0 and goes into `/feed.json` by default.
2. **Before the first deploy**, decide whether it should get a DOI. A DOI can't be withdrawn, and Rogue Scholar keeps an archived copy, so get it right before it ships.
   - Hold it out of DOIs: `archive: false` in the frontmatter (it stays CC BY).
   - Keep all rights: `license: "all-rights-reserved"` (no notice, no DOI). Use this for poems or anything that may go to a journal.
   - Rogue Scholar declines posts primarily generated by AI. A post drafted by a model and published as drafted gets `archive: false`.
3. Every factual claim in the post must match the proof bank. The DOI freezes it.
4. Within a day or so of the deploy, check that the post has a DOI on Rogue Scholar and that it has reached ORCID.
5. Add the DOI to the post's proof-bank entry, or create one.

## Each new software release

1. Keep `CITATION.cff` current in the repository, with his ORCID iD.
2. Publish the GitHub release. Zenodo archives it and mints a DOI.
3. Check that ORCID shows it, then add the DOI to the project's proof-bank entry and to the project's site page.

## Each new outside credit

1. Wait until the outside record is public (advisory, recording, byline).
2. Add it to `/work/` on the site, and to the proof bank with the outside link.
3. Add an entry to `orcid/works.bib` and import that one entry, or add it on ORCID by hand.

## What never goes in

- Anything the proof bank lists as unverified or do-not-claim.
- Acknowledgements in someone else's paper or dissertation: they are evidence, not works.
- Private repositories or undisclosed findings.
